Party Math

The 46 guests at 8 a table take 6 tables, leaving 2 empty seats.

Boundary: arithmetic only Party Math counts party cups, tables and trays with multiplication, division and rounding-up: the cups and packs a guest list takes, the tables, the trays of food, and the cost. It uses no named constants. It applies the counting as written, so it has not met your guests and does not know who will come, who will eat what, your venue or any food safety or capacity rule, and it gives no verdict that the food is enough or the room fits. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: 46 guests, 3 cups each in packs of 50, 8 a table, 4 servings each in trays of 24, packs at $6.25 and trays at $14.00.

The tables truth

The 46 guests at 8 a table take 6 tables, leaving 2 empty seats.

The cups and trays truths

The 46 guests at 3 cups each need 138 cups, in packs of 50 that is 3 packs, leaving 12 cups. The 46 guests at 4 servings each need 184 servings, in trays of 24 that is 8 trays, leaving 8 servings.

The cost truth

The 3 packs at $6.25 and 8 trays at $14 cost $130.75, packs $18.75 and trays $112.00.
